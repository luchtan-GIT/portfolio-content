I have been a songwriter for most of my life. It is one of the most exhilarating and profound ways I can express myself, emotionally and musically. When I am my best self, I have a practice — working on and refining an evolving set of songs as I progress through my life. Often, I look for characters or experiences that, for one reason or another, struck a resonant tone in some part of my process, and I explore them, perhaps exaggerate them, perhaps change them.

I was a member of ASCAP, but I was such a proponent of open source at the time — there was a lot of talk about how knowledge needed to be free, about the public domain — that I pulled myself out of it. I posted my stuff on what was one of the first platforms where you could put up Creative Commons licensed music. Given the opportunity to make money over the years — well, it's always slow coming, and from my career I never would have made very much through ASCAP anyway — but I've made four figures in total, and I'm happy with that.

Because I did some co-writing with a songwriter here in Catalonia, I joined the European songwriting association. Times are different now. Creative Commons is more prevalent, the idea of public domain is much better understood, and I am much less of a revolutionary. I've learned to not be so rigid in my thinking.
